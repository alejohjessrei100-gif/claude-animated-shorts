# "The Skyscraper That Cooked a Car" — original script v2 (2026-11-13, trimmed for length)
Format: ~60 s vertical short, ~3.7 words/s, one steady narrator voice, our own characters + story. No captions.

## Facts (sources)
- 20 Fenchurch Street ("Walkie-Talkie"), City of London, architect Rafael Viñoly; concave south face focused sunlight
  onto Eastcheap in late summer 2013 while the tower was still being finished. Nickname "Walkie Scorchie" / "Fryscraper".
  (globalnews.ca/news/816390; building.co.uk/news/walkie-talkie-tower-is-melting-cars/5059956.article;
  nationalgeographic.com 130904-walkie-talkie-building-london-melts-sunlight-physics-science)
- A businessman parked his Jaguar XJ for about an hour across the street; came back to a melted wing mirror, badge and
  warped panels. A van was also damaged. Developers paid for the repairs and suspended nearby parking bays.
  (ITV 2013-09-03; Channel 4 3 Sep 2013; Yahoo News "London driver says skyscraper melted his car")
- 91.3 °C measured at street level on Eastcheap. City A.M. reporter Jim Waterson fried an egg in a pan in the beam.
  (vitruvius.com.br drops 14.072; cityam.com; londonist.com 2013/09)
- The hot spot moved with the sun and lasted only part of each day; temporary scaffold screen, then permanent
  horizontal aluminium fins across the south face (2014). (todayifoundout.com 2015/08 melting-cars-walkie-talkie)
- Same architect's curved Vdara hotel, Las Vegas, was reported in 2010 to focus a "death ray" on its pool, singeing
  sunbathers and melting plastic bags; fixed with film and big umbrellas. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vdara; abc7 archive)

## Script
In 2013, a skyscraper melted a car, without ever touching it.
It's a hot September, and a shiny new tower is rising in the middle of London. Locals call it the Walkie-Talkie.
One morning, a man parks his brand new Jaguar across the street and heads off to work.
An hour later, he comes back. The mirror has drooped like an ice cream. The badge has slid right off. The side of the car is bubbling.
A thermometer on the pavement hits ninety-one degrees.
A reporter brings a frying pan, cracks an egg onto it, and the egg actually fries.
Luckily, the sun moves across the sky, so the hot spot keeps moving.
Unluckily, the sun moves across the sky, so the hot spot keeps moving.
You see, the tower's glass front is curved inward, like the inside of a giant spoon.
Ever burned a leaf with a magnifying glass? It grabs sunshine from a big area and squeezes it into one tiny spot.
A curved wall of mirrors does the exact same trick, just thirty-seven floors tall.
The owners paid for the car, then covered the whole front in metal fins, like a giant sun visor.
And the architect? Three years earlier, his curved hotel in Las Vegas had already been cooking sunbathers by the pool.
