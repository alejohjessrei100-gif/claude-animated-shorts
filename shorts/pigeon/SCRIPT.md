## Facts (checked 2026-11-07)
- Project Pigeon: B. F. Skinner's WWII pigeon-guided glide bomb (the "Pelican"). The National Defense Research Committee,
  though sceptical, funded it with $25,000 (1943). [Wikipedia "Project Pigeon"; Task & Purpose]
- Pigeons were trained by operant conditioning (food rewards) to peck at an image of a target. A lens in the nose projected
  the target onto a screen; off-centre pecks tilted the screen, which moved the bomb's controls. [Wikipedia]
- Three pigeons per bomb, steering by majority rule. [Wikipedia; Task & Purpose]
- Tests went well; the programme was cancelled on 8 October 1944 because it would "delay others ... of more immediate
  promise". Skinner: "our problem was no one would take us seriously". [Wikipedia; Skinner, "Pigeons in a Pelican", 1960]
- The Navy revived it as Project Orcon (1948-1953), cancelled when electronic guidance proved reliable. (not in the script)
- 2024 Ig Nobel Peace Prize awarded to Skinner (posthumously) for Project Pigeon; his daughter Julie Vargas accepted it.
  1944 -> 2024 = 80 years. [Wikipedia; Ig Nobel 2024]
- "Pigeons? Really?" is a dramatised line (reference style); the scepticism itself is documented.
