# Roblox-style story Shorts: 10 script options (pick one)

Format measured on @kalemod's top 15 (docs/metrics_table.md): ~60 s, ~250-280 words read fast (~257 wpm), first word at
0.0 s, **third person**, no pauses. 8/15 open "In this world, [rule]." (a misjudged hero, a twist that re-reads the rule);
6/15 are karma stories (a cruel line, years pass, the same line comes back). All premises, characters and lines below are
original and were checked against the channel's 157 titles. Each one makes its rule visible on screen (skill tip #1).

---

## 1. The Floating Girl 🎈 (rule)
*On screen: sad people hover; Pip drifts a foot up on a string; she rises over a flood.*

In this world, sadness makes you lighter. Have a bad day and your feet lift an inch off the ground. Lose a pet and you float above your bed for a week. Grown-ups carry bricks in their pockets after funerals just to stay down. And nobody floated higher than Pip. Ever since her dad left, she drifted a whole foot above the ground. Her mom tied a string to her backpack so she wouldn't blow away on windy days. Kids called her Balloon Girl. They'd tug her string in the hallway and watch her bob. Somebody taped her to the ceiling once, and the whole class laughed. So Pip stopped going to recess. Then one afternoon, the river flooded. Water rushed through the streets so fast that cars started floating. A little boy climbed onto the roof of a bus stop, and the water kept rising. The fire truck's ladder wasn't long enough. The rescue boat couldn't fight the current. Grown-ups screamed from the hill, but nobody could reach him. Pip looked at the boy. Then at the string on her backpack. She thought about her dad. About every lunch she ate alone. About every tug on that string. And she let herself feel all of it at once. She untied the string. Her feet left the ground, and she rose over the water, higher than the rooftops, straight to the bus stop. She grabbed the boy's hand and floated him back to the hill. The whole town was waiting. They hugged her so hard she started laughing. And for the first time in years, Pip's feet touched the ground.

## 2. The Frozen Boy ❄️ (rule)
*On screen: warm kids glow orange, lonely kids frost over; Eli walks through a fire in a cloud of steam.*

In this world, your temperature shows how loved you are. Kids with big families feel warm like fresh bread. Popular kids are basically heaters. And lonely people freeze. Eli was the coldest kid in school. Frost grew on his sleeves. His breath fogged up indoors. If he touched a juice box, it froze solid. Nobody would sit next to him, and the teacher moved his desk by the window so his frost wouldn't fog up the whole classroom. Kids dared each other to high-five him and ran away shaking their hands. On the coldest morning of winter, the whole school packed into the gym for the assembly. Then the heater in the music room caught fire. Smoke poured down the hallway. Three little kids were stuck behind a wall of flames, and the firefighters couldn't get through. Everyone ran for the doors. Everyone except Eli. He walked straight into the fire. The flames hissed and turned to steam around him. Frost spread across the floor in front of every step. He found the three kids crying in the corner, took their hands, and walked them out through the smoke while the fire died behind him. Outside, the fire chief stared at him. He asked how a kid could walk through fire without a single burn. Eli didn't answer. He was too busy noticing something he'd never felt before. The three kids were still holding his hands. They wouldn't let go. And for the first time in his whole life, Eli's hands were warm.

## 3. The Pushing Boy 🧲 (rule)
*On screen: kind people pull things toward them, Dax pushes everything away; he stops a runaway truck with his palms.*

In this world, everyone is a magnet. Kind people pull things toward them. Friends, good luck, even stray cats. Selfish people push everything away. And nobody pushed harder than Dax. His food slid off his plate. Doors swung shut in his face. Balls flew away before he could catch them. When he walked down the hallway, kids stepped back without even knowing why. Everyone decided he must be the most selfish kid in school. Parents told their kids to stay away. Teachers sat him alone in the back. Every night his mom told him the same thing. You'll understand it one day. Dax didn't believe her. Then came field day. The whole school was out on the playground when a delivery truck lost its brakes at the top of the hill. It came rolling down, faster and faster, straight toward the little kids by the swings. Teachers screamed. Nobody could move fast enough. Dax ran. He planted his feet in front of the swings, closed his eyes, and held out both hands. The truck slowed. Then it stopped, an inch from his palms. Then it rolled all the way back up the hill. The playground went silent. Then everyone ran to him. Kids hugged him. Teachers cried. And for the first time, nothing pushed them away. That night, his mom finally told him the truth she'd been saving. Dax was never pushing things away from himself. He was pushing them away from everyone else.

## 4. The Snail Partner 🐌 (rule)
*On screen: lions, owls and cheetahs walk beside their kids; Ollie's snail leaves a glowing trail in a pitch-black cave.*

In this world, on your tenth birthday an animal walks out of the forest and chooses you. Brave kids get lions. Smart kids get owls. Fast kids get cheetahs. Whatever chooses you, that's who you are for the rest of your life. On Ollie's birthday, the whole family waited at the edge of the forest. They waited all morning. Then all afternoon. Then right before sunset, a snail crawled out. The kids at school never let him forget it. They called him Slime Boy. He was late for everything because his partner took ten minutes to cross a hallway. Teachers stopped calling on him. Only his grandpa smiled about it. He told Ollie, the slow ones always remember the way. Then came the class trip to the caves. Halfway in, the ground shook. Rocks fell, and every light went out. It was so dark that kids couldn't see their own hands. The lions roared at nothing. The owls couldn't see either. The cheetahs ran in circles and got everyone more lost. Kids started crying. The teacher couldn't find the way out. Then Ollie looked down. Behind his snail, all the way back to the entrance, was a thin silver trail. Glowing in the dark. Ollie picked up his snail and said, follow me. He walked the whole class out, one step at a time, along the shining line. When they reached the sunlight, every kid turned to look at the snail. It was never slow. It was the only one that remembered the way back.

## 5. The Glowing Footprints 👣 (rule)
*On screen: every step glows on the ground for a day; Ivy's trail lights a path through the dark woods.*

In this world, every step you take glows on the ground for a whole day, so everybody always knows where you've been. Kids can't sneak out. Thieves get caught by breakfast. And every morning, the whole town talked about Ivy. Because every single night, her footprints went out of town, past the last streetlight, and straight into the woods. Nobody knew why. People said she was stealing. Some said she was meeting someone she shouldn't. Parents told their kids to stay away from her, and the kids at school called her the Night Walker. Ivy never explained. She just kept walking. Then came the first big snowstorm of winter. A little boy wandered out to look at the snow and didn't come back. The snow covered his footprints in minutes. Search teams went into the woods with flashlights, but the storm was so thick they could barely see each other, and two of them got lost too. Ivy ran to the edge of the woods and pointed. Her trail was still there. Not just from last night, but from every night before, stacked on top of each other, so bright it lit up the snow like a road. The whole town followed it. At the end of the glowing path was the old ranger cabin, and inside, wrapped in a blanket, sat the little boy. He said he saw the light and followed it. Ivy's little brother got lost in those woods two years ago. Everyone thought she was sneaking out. She was building a way home.

## 6. The Thousand Smiles 🙂 (rule)
*On screen: a counter on every wrist; Rosie's still reads 1000; it drops to zero while she keeps smiling.*

In this world, you only get a thousand smiles in your whole life. A little counter on your wrist shows how many you have left. People save them for weddings and new babies. Some kids burn through half of theirs before they turn ten. And then there was Rosie. Twelve years old, and her counter still said one thousand. She had never smiled. Not at birthday parties. Not at puppies. Not even when the whole class threw her a surprise party. Kids called her Stoneface. They told jokes right in front of her just to watch nothing happen. Her mom worried. Her teachers worried. Even her grandma asked her what she was saving them for. Rosie only said, you'll see. Then her little brother got sick. He was in the hospital for weeks, and his counter had been stuck at zero since the day he was born. The doctors said he'd never smiled once. On his birthday, Rosie walked into his room, sat on his bed, and smiled. Then she smiled again. And again. She told him every joke she had been saving. She made faces until the nurses started laughing too. Her counter dropped. Nine hundred. Five hundred. One hundred. Zero. And she was still smiling. Then her little brother looked at her, and for the first time in his life, the corners of his mouth went up. The whole hospital went quiet. Nobody had ever noticed it before. The counter was never counting your smiles. It was only counting the ones you kept.

## 7. The Loud Heart 🥁 (rule)
*On screen: a nervous heartbeat shows as rings in the air; Theo's booms across the fog and echoes off the cliffs.*

In this world, when you get nervous, everyone can hear your heartbeat. A little nervous and the people next to you hear it. Really scared and the whole room does. Liars get caught in seconds. And nobody had a louder heart than Theo. It boomed every time a teacher called his name. Every time he raised his hand. Every time somebody looked at him for too long. Kids called him Drum. They'd scare him on purpose just to hear it echo down the hallway. He stopped trying out for anything. He stopped talking. Then came the class ferry trip across the bay. Halfway across, a fog rolled in so thick you couldn't see the front of the boat. Then the lighthouse went dark. The captain couldn't see the rocks. The radio only crackled. Somewhere close, waves were smashing against the cliffs. Kids started crying. Theo was terrified. His heart was pounding so loud the whole deck could hear it. Then the captain held up his hand and said, listen. Every heartbeat was bouncing back from the fog. Off the cliffs. One echo on the left. Nothing on the right. The captain steered toward the silence. Theo held onto the railing, scared out of his mind, his heart booming like thunder, while the captain followed the echoes all the way past the rocks and into the harbor. When the fog lifted, the whole town was standing on the dock. Everybody's heart was pounding on that boat. Theo's was just the only one loud enough to bring them home.

## 8. The Lunch Tray 🍽️ (karma)
*On screen: a tray dumped in the cafeteria; years later the same boy in a chef's coat hands over an apron.*

A rich kid dumped his whole lunch tray on a quiet boy in front of the entire cafeteria. Then he pointed at the boy's mom behind the counter, in her hairnet, and said, some people are born to clean up after others. Everybody laughed. The boy's mom came out and wiped the food off his shirt, and she told him something he never forgot. Anyone can cook. Not everyone learns how. So he learned. Every night after school, he stayed in that kitchen with her. He washed dishes at a diner when he was fifteen. He burned his hands so many times he stopped counting. He slept in the back of restaurants just to watch the chefs work. Fifteen years later, he opened his own restaurant, and people waited three months for a table. Then one day, a new guy showed up to interview for a job. Dishwasher. His family's company had gone bankrupt. He'd lost the house, the car, and every friend who used to laugh at his jokes. He walked into the kitchen, looked up, and froze. The head chef was the boy from the cafeteria. The whole kitchen went quiet. The man waited for him to laugh. To tell him to get out. Instead, the chef walked over, took a clean apron off the hook, and tied it around the man's waist himself. Then he leaned in and said quietly, some people are born to clean up after others. The rest of us learn. Welcome to the team.

## 9. The Broken Glasses 👓 (karma)
*On screen: glasses snapped in a hallway; years later a surgeon removes the bandages from the same bully's eyes.*

A boy snapped a girl's glasses in half in the middle of the hallway. She couldn't see a thing. She was on her knees feeling for the pieces while everyone laughed. And he said, doesn't matter, nobody wants to look at you anyway. She taped those glasses back together and wore them for the rest of the year. She decided that if she couldn't fix how people saw her, she'd fix how they saw. She studied every night. She skipped parties. She read about eyes until she could draw one from memory. Twelve years later, she was the youngest eye surgeon in the city. And one morning, a new patient came in. A man whose eyesight was disappearing. In a few months, he'd be completely blind. Every other doctor had said there was nothing they could do. She looked at his name on the chart, and she recognized it right away. He didn't recognize her at all. She spent nine hours on his surgery. The nurses said she never once took a break. Two days later, she slowly took the bandages off his eyes. He blinked. Everything was blurry. Then it was clear. The first thing he saw was her face, smiling down at him. He started crying. He told her she was the most beautiful thing he'd ever seen. Then he noticed the taped-up glasses sitting on her desk. And she said, doesn't matter. Nobody wants to look at me anyway.

## 10. The Last Pick 🧯 (karma)
*On screen: the smallest kid always picked last in gym; years later he squeezes through an attic window in a fire.*

A gym teacher made the smallest kid in class pick last every single day. Then he'd blow his whistle and tell everyone, small guys don't save anybody. The kids laughed. They started saying it in the hallway. They wrote it on his locker. He heard it so many times he started to believe it. But he never stopped showing up. He ran every morning before school. He did push-ups until his arms shook. He got rejected from the fire academy twice for being too small. The third time, they let him in, and he was the smallest firefighter in the whole station. The others called him Pocket. Then one night, a house on the edge of town caught fire. A little girl was stuck in the attic, and the only way in was a tiny window under the roof. The big firefighters tried. Their shoulders wouldn't fit. The roof was starting to fall. Pocket took off his tank, squeezed through that window, and disappeared into the smoke. The whole street held its breath. Ten seconds. Twenty. Then he came climbing back out with the little girl in his arms. An old man was screaming her name on the lawn. Her grandpa. The gym teacher. He recognized the firefighter right away. He couldn't say a word. Pocket walked over, put the little girl in her grandpa's arms, and said, small guys don't save anybody, right? Then he turned around and went back in for the dog.
