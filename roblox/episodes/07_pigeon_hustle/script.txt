In my town, every time you complain, a pigeon lands on you. And it won't leave until you say something nice. Grumpy people walk around looking like statues in the park. So when I was twelve, I did the smart thing. I started selling compliments. One dollar each, read out loud, and the pigeon flies away. Business was slow. A kid who hated broccoli. A dad stuck in traffic. Then my neighbor Tessa started giving compliments for free. So I needed more complaining. I opened a lemonade stand next to the bus stop. No sugar. Extra lemons. One sip, and everyone complained. Pigeon. Pigeon. Pigeon. Then came the heat wave. The air conditioners broke. The pool closed. The ice cream truck ran out of ice cream. By Friday, the whole town was covered in birds, and I had sold four hundred compliments. I bought a new bike. Then a better bike. Then a helmet with spikes, just in case. On Saturday, the mayor called me up on stage. He said I had made this town kinder all week, and he gave me a medal. The whole crowd clapped. Tessa just stared at me. I looked at the medal and said, really? It's not even real gold. And every pigeon in town turned around.
