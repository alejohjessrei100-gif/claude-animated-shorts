In this world, sadness makes you lighter. Have a bad day and your feet lift an inch off the ground. Lose a pet and you float above your bed for a week. Grown-ups carry bricks in their pockets after funerals just to stay down. And nobody floated higher than Pip. Ever since her dad left, she drifted a whole foot above the ground. Her mom tied a string to her backpack so she wouldn't blow away on windy days. Kids called her Balloon Girl. They'd tug her string in the hallway and watch her bob. Somebody taped her to the ceiling once, and the whole class laughed. So Pip stopped going to recess. Then one afternoon, the river flooded. Water rushed through the streets so fast that cars started floating. A little boy climbed onto the roof of a bus stop, and the water kept rising. The fire truck's ladder wasn't long enough. The rescue boat couldn't fight the current. Grown-ups screamed from the hill, but nobody could reach him. Pip looked at the boy. Then at the string on her backpack. She thought about her dad. About every lunch she ate alone. About every tug on that string. And she let herself feel all of it at once. She untied the string. Her feet left the ground, and she rose over the water, higher than the rooftops, straight to the bus stop. She grabbed the boy's hand and floated him back to the hill. The whole town was waiting. They hugged her so hard she started laughing. And for the first time in years, Pip's feet touched the ground.
